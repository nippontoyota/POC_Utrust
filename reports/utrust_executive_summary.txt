This document provides highly detailed answers regarding how the UTrust system handles unexpected conflicts, malicious actions, human errors, and edge cases. Every single answer is backed by a corresponding automated database test.

================================================================================
PART 1: PURCHASE OFFICER (PO) ASSIGNMENT & WORKFLOW
================================================================================

Q: If two Sales Officers in the exact same branch hit "Submit" on two different cars at the exact same millisecond, what will happen? Will the system overload a single Purchase Officer?
A: The system acts like an incredibly fast traffic cop. It freezes the queue for a fraction of a millisecond, gives the first car to the Purchase Officer who has been waiting longest, and then immediately gives the second car to the next available Purchase Officer. The system physically prevents one person from getting both cases simultaneously out of turn.
[Core Detail: Handled via PostgreSQL "FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED" row-level locking. Validated by Test EC1.1: Two simultaneous submissions get DIFFERENT POs.]

Q: If a new branch opens and Sales Officers start submitting cars, but the HR department hasn't officially activated any Purchase Officers for that branch yet, what will happen? Will the cars get stuck in limbo forever?
A: The database will instantly reject the submission. The car will safely remain in "Draft" mode, and the Sales Officer will see an error: "No active Purchase Officer available in this branch." Once a Purchase Officer is officially hired, the car can be successfully submitted.
[Core Detail: Handled via the assignment engine scanning active profiles before saving. Validated by Test EC18.1: Branch with no PO fails gracefully.]

Q: If a Sales Officer is unhappy with the PO assigned by the round-robin system and tries to forcefully change the PO's name in the database, what will happen?
A: It is physically impossible. Sales Officers do not have the database permissions required to manually change the assigned PO. Any attempt to bypass the round-robin engine is rejected by the database.
[Core Detail: Handled via Column-Level Grant Enforcement. Validated by Test EC13.1: SO cannot directly set assigned_po_id.]

Q: If a Purchase Officer from the Trivandrum branch somehow finds the ID of a car in the Kochi branch and tries to submit an evaluation for it, what will happen?
A: The system will block them. Purchase Officers are strictly confined to evaluating cars within their own specific branch.
[Core Detail: Handled via strict Row-Level Security (RLS). Validated by Test EC4.4: PO from another branch cannot start evaluation.]

Q: If a Purchase Officer forgets to physically inspect the car and tries to just submit a random offer price to save time, what will happen?
A: The system will block the offer. The PO is mathematically forced to log a "Start Evaluation / Inspection Completed" event before the database will accept an offer price.
[Core Detail: Handled via a PL/pgSQL BEFORE INSERT trigger on the evaluation tables. Validated by Test EC4.1: PO cannot submit without confirming inspection.]

Q: If a Purchase Officer accidentally clicks the "Start Evaluation" button twice in a row because their internet lagged, what will happen? Will it ruin the audit logs?
A: The database simply ignores the second click. It is mathematically "idempotent," meaning it securely logs the start time exactly once and completely discards any duplicate clicks for the same car.
[Core Detail: `start_po_evaluation` uses an `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` safeguard. Validated by Test EC4.3: start_po_evaluation is idempotent.]

PART 2: DATA INTEGRITY, MANDATORY FIELDS & DUPLICATE PREVENTION
================================================================================

Q: If a Sales Officer accidentally (or intentionally) types in the Registration Number of a car (e.g., KL01AB1234) that is already being evaluated by the team, what will happen?
A: The system will instantly block the Sales Officer from even creating the draft. It enforces a strict "One Active Case Per Vehicle" rule across the entire database to prevent duplicate work.
[Core Detail: Handled by the `uniq_open_vehicle_reg` partial unique index. Validated by Test EC2.9: Duplicate vehicle reg blocked at draft INSERT.]

Q: If the customer rejected our offer last month and the old case was closed, and now the Sales Officer tries to submit that same KL01 Registration Number again today, what will happen?
A: The system will allow it. It is smart enough to know that the old case is officially "Dead" (Closed, Withdrawn, or Rejected), so it automatically frees up that registration number for a fresh, new case.
[Core Detail: The unique index actively excludes terminal statuses. Validated by Test EC16.1: Same reg number reused after previous case closed.]

Q: If a Sales Officer is in a massive rush and hits "Submit" with only 3 photos uploaded instead of the mandatory 6 angles, what will happen?
A: The database acts as a strict gatekeeper. It actively counts the photos attached to the case ID. If it doesn't see the minimum required categories (front, rear, interior, etc.), it blocks the submission entirely and forces the SO to upload the rest.
[Core Detail: The `submit_case_for_evaluation` RPC runs a COUNT() grouped by category. Validated by Test 2.3: Submit with < 6 photos throws error.]

Q: If a Sales Officer forgets to type in the Odometer reading or the Customer's Expected Price, but tries to submit the car anyway, what will happen?
A: The database physically requires all mandatory fields to be non-null. The moment the case tries to transition from Draft to Pending, the database throws a "Missing required fields" error.
[Core Detail: Enforced via `status` transition checks in PL/pgSQL. Validated by Test 2.2: Submit with missing fields throws error.]

Q: If a Purchase Officer accidentally typos the offer price and types ₹0 or a negative number, what will happen?
A: The database will instantly reject the entry. Offer prices and counter-offers must logically be greater than zero.
[Core Detail: Enforced via CHECK constraints on numeric columns. Validated by Test 5.1/5.2: Negative and zero offer prices rejected.]

Q: If a customer demands an absurdly high counter-offer like ₹5,000 Crores, or tries to write a 10-page essay in the counter-offer note box, what will happen?
A: The system automatically blocks it. The database enforces a maximum numeric limit (₹999.99 Crores) to prevent math overflow errors, and strictly limits text notes to 1,000 characters to prevent database bloat.
[Core Detail: Hardcoded `NUMERIC(14,2)` precision and string length limits. Validated by Test EC5.4 & EC5.5: Absurd prices and lengths rejected.]

Q: If a lazy employee is asked to provide a "Cancellation Reason" and they just hit the Spacebar, press the 'Tab' key, or hit 'Enter' a few times to bypass the text box, what will happen?
A: The database will instantly strip away the invisible spaces and reject the cancellation entirely. It will throw an error demanding actual, readable text. Management will never receive a blank reason.
[Core Detail: Uses deep string inspection `trim(both E' \t\n\r')`. Validated by Test EC9.2 & EC9.3: Withdraw with tab/newline rejected.]

Q: If the company legally needs to prove that cases are numbered perfectly without skipping any digits (e.g., UT-KOC-202610-0001, 0002, 0003), what happens if ten cars are submitted simultaneously?
A: The sequence is guaranteed to be gapless and perfectly ordered. The database uses a secure, atomic locking counter specific to each branch and month, ensuring no reference numbers are ever skipped or duplicated even under extreme load.
[Core Detail: `generate_case_ref()` relies on an atomic `UPDATE ... RETURNING` sequence table. Validated by Test EC10.2: Sequences are gapless.]

PART 3: TERMINAL STATES & THE POINT OF NO RETURN
================================================================================

Q: If a car has been sold and the case is marked "Closed", and an employee later tries to go back into the system to alter the offer price, or cancel the deal to hide an error, what will happen?
A: The system will block the edit and log the failed attempt. Once a case reaches a final state (like Closed or Cancelled), it becomes permanently locked in stone. No one can change the numbers or withdraw the case after the fact.
[Core Detail: Handled by Terminal State Immutability triggers. Validated by Test EC6.1 - EC6.6: Cannot withdraw/close/cancel a closed case.]

Q: If a customer accepts the offer, but 10 minutes later changes their mind and wants to submit a counter-offer instead, what will happen?
A: The system blocks it. Counter-offers can only be submitted while the case is actively awaiting the customer's decision. Once accepted, the deal automatically moves to the purchase completion phase and locks out negotiations.
[Core Detail: Handled by strict status transition guards. Validated by Test 2.11: Counter offer after customer accepted throws error.]

Q: If a customer rejects our offer, but the Sales Officer forgets to ask if we can list it on the broker marketplace, what will happen?
A: The system defaults to protecting the customer. If broker consent is left blank or missing, the database automatically assumes "No" and formally kills the case (Status: `rejected_not_listed`) without ever sending it to external brokers.
[Core Detail: Default boolean guards in `record_customer_decision`. Validated by Test EC14.7: Null broker consent defaults to rejected_not_listed.]

PART 4: BROKER MARKETPLACE & AUCTION FAIRNESS
================================================================================

Q: If two external brokers see a highly desirable car on the app and hit the "Submit Offer" button at the exact same fraction of a second, what will happen?
A: The system will force one bid to wait a fraction of a millisecond while the first bid is processed. This completely prevents race conditions, double-bookings, or phantom bids. The market is strictly first-come, first-served at the millisecond level.
[Core Detail: PostgreSQL transaction isolation guarantees serializability for concurrent INSERTS. Validated by Test EC9 & Broker Flow checks.]

Q: If an external broker writes a script (a "bot") to rapidly read our internal database to spy on what other competitor brokers are bidding, what will happen?
A: The broker will see exactly zero rows of competitor data. Brokers are strictly sandboxed; they can only ever query and see the bids they personally submitted. They are completely blind to competitor bids.
[Core Detail: `broker_offers` table enforces a strict RLS policy checking `broker_id = auth.uid()`. Validated by Test EC17.3: Broker cannot read raw broker_offers table.]

Q: If a broker reserves a car but falls asleep and forgets to finish the payment, what will happen? Is the car locked forever?
A: No. The reservation acts as a strict temporary countdown timer. Once the hold time expires, the database automatically kicks the broker out, deletes the reservation, and throws the car back onto the open marketplace for other brokers to bid on.
[Core Detail: Real-time temporal queries filter out expired holds (`expires_at > now()`). Validated by Broker Flow: Reservation Expiration.]

Q: If an external broker is looking at cars on the app, and a Sales Officer accidentally marks a photo of the customer's RC Book (or a photo clearly showing the license plate) as "Visible to Brokers", what will happen?
A: The customer's privacy remains perfectly safe. The database strictly overrides the Sales Officer. It completely forbids RC Books or photos flagged with visible plates from ever being shown on the external marketplace.
[Core Detail: Hardcoded security logic in the `broker_cases` view filters out `rc_book` and `is_plate_visible = true`. Validated by Test EC15.1 & EC15.2.]

Q: If a broker's account gets officially suspended by management due to bad behavior, but they still have the app open on their phone, what will happen if they try to bid?
A: The moment they click bid, the database checks their exact, real-time approval status. Since they are suspended, the system instantly throws a "Permission Denied" error and kicks them out of the marketplace.
[Core Detail: RLS policies enforce `is_approved_broker() = true` on every single read and write. Validated by Test EC8.1 & EC8.2.]

PART 5: SECURITY, IMPERSONATION, & AUDIT TAMPERING
================================================================================

Q: If a staff member secretly creates an external "Broker" account using their personal phone number to try and bid on company cars for cheap, what will happen?
A: The system will completely lock them out of the external auction. If the database detects that a broker account also belongs to an internal employee, the employee badge overrides everything. They are physically walled off from viewing the auction board or placing bids.
[Core Detail: The `is_approved_broker()` function explicitly returns false if an internal profile exists. Validated by Test EC7.1 & EC7.2.]

Q: If a Sales Officer in the Kochi branch tries to secretly look up the pricing or customer details of a car submitted in the Kozhikode branch, what will happen?
A: The database will return a completely blank screen. We use an invisibility cloak between branches. To a Kochi employee, Kozhikode's data literally does not exist. Only authorized Group Managers can see across branches.
[Core Detail: RLS policies enforce `branch_id = user_branch_id`. Validated by Test EC2.1 & EC2.3.]

Q: If a Branch Manager in Trivandrum tries to approve a new external broker who just signed up for the platform, what will happen?
A: They will get a permission denied error. Only Group Managers possess the global database authority required to approve, vet, and manage external brokers for the platform. Branch Managers can only manage their own staff and cases.
[Core Detail: Only `is_group_manager = true` bypasses the broker management RLS. Validated by Test 6.2: Branch Manager cannot manage brokers.]

Q: If a Sales Officer from Branch A tries to trick the system by writing code to guess the Image URL of a car's photos from Branch B, what will happen?
A: They will see broken images or permission errors. The security rules extend downward to every single photo attached to a car. If you can't see the parent car, the database explicitly blocks you from downloading its photos.
[Core Detail: `case_photos` RLS policies automatically inherit visibility from the parent `cases` table. Validated by Test EC2.6 & EC2.7.]

Q: If a Sales Officer gets fired mid-shift, and another Sales Officer tries to step in and cancel their pending cases, what will happen?
A: The system will block the second Sales Officer. Cases are strictly tied to their original creator. Even colleagues in the exact same branch cannot hijack, cancel, or modify another person's case without managerial override.
[Core Detail: RLS policies mandate `sales_officer_id = auth.uid()` for all destructive actions. Validated by Test EC3.1 - EC3.5: Impersonation blocked.]

Q: If an employee claims "I didn't click that button, the system must have glitched," or if they try to go into the system logs to delete the entry to hide their tracks, what happens?
A: First, they will receive a "Permission Denied" error if they try to delete the log; the Audit Log is strictly "Append-Only." Second, the database automatically records who did the action based on their secure login token, so no one can forge someone else's name. The logs are permanent and indisputable.
[Core Detail: `case_events` table lacks UPDATE/DELETE grants for staff. Actor attribution is forced via `auth.uid()`. Validated by Test EC11.6 & EC17.1.]

Q: If a clever Sales Officer decides they want to artificially manipulate the sequential Case Reference numbers (e.g., UT-KOC-202610-0001) by typing in their own custom code, what will happen?
A: The database simply ignores them. Case Reference numbers are generated automatically and securely by the backend database the exact moment the car is submitted. Sales Officers have zero ability to type or alter them.
[Core Detail: Column-level REVOKE rules prevent direct writes to `case_ref`. Validated by Test EC10.1 & EC13.3.]
